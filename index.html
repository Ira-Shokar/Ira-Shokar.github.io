About

I work at NVIDIA on probabilistic neural emulators of fluid dynamics, with a focus on weather and climate modelling and aerodynamic engineering. My work spans research and application of AI models for physical systems, from probabilistic weather forecasting and Earth observation to aerodynamic prediction from CAD, with Earth-2 and AIE.

I am particularly interested in physical inductive biases for neural architectures (latent rollout models), optimisation and loss design for probabilistic modelling and uncertainty quantification (scoring rules, spectral losses), generalisation, and modelling extreme or rare events. If you would like to collaborate on any of these topics, do reach out: ishokar at nvidia.com.

Key research areas:

  • CRPS and rollout stability: CRPS loss, latent transformers and spectral regularisation give stable rollouts over 105 steps that match regime statistics.
  • Latent rollouts: autoregressive rollout fully in latent space, and the encode/process/decode losses needed for stability.
  • Scenario conditioning: conditioning on PDE parameters via AdaLN enables zero-shot interpolation across unseen forcing regimes.
  • Out-of-distribution and extreme events: emulators can forecast regimes and extreme events absent from training, and I characterise when this succeeds or fails.
